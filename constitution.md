# The Founding Charter

*Nothing in this document is in force until ratified as Act 1.*

## Preamble

This community rests on two constructs.

The first is ordinary: we are friends, and this server exists for the ordinary business of friendship conducted over Discord: conversation, games, food, pets, and keeping up with one another.

The second is an experiment: this server has no moderators and no administrators. The members govern themselves entirely by vote: on decisions, on thresholds, and on the rules of voting itself. We are our own parliament. The server will grow as we decide, including, if we so decide, not at all.

We are aware that the experiment is imperfect and that its guarantees are social rather than technical. We consider this acceptable. The experiment is run for its own sake.

## Article I: The Community and the Fork Doctrine

The server is not the community. The community is the people, whose relationships exist independently of this platform.

Should the server be destroyed, whether by a rogue owner, a compromised account, or Discord itself, the community will reconstitute elsewhere, without whoever destroyed it.

The server's structure, rules, registry, this constitution, and the Records are backed up off-server by the bot. The only asset genuinely at risk is message history. We accept this exposure knowingly.

Because the worst case is recoverable, trust is inexpensive here. Policy should be written accordingly. The foundation is stronger than its weakest flaw.

## Ownership

The platform requires that a single account own the server. A permanent
owner would contradict the spirit of this document; the contradiction
cannot be engineered away, only governed. Ownership is therefore an
elected office, transferred in fact to whoever wins, and the founder claims
no more right to it than any other citizen. The owner's duty is to not
exercise their powers.

## The founding sequence

- **Act 1**: ratification of this charter.
- **Act 2**: the first ownership election.
- **Everything after**: the parliament writes its own law. How proposals
  work, what passes and with how many votes, how elections recur, how
  votes about people are protected, and whatever else it sees fit.
  Until then, one provisional rule applies: a proposal passes when more
  citizens vote yes than no.

To set the machinery in motion, the founder seeds the server before any
vote exists: the channels, the clerk, and provisional rules of procedure.
These arrangements claim no consent and carry no authority beyond getting
us started. Ratifying this charter accepts them as a starting point,
nothing more; any of them can be overwritten by a later Act.
