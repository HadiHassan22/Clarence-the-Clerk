# The Charter

*Draft. In force provisionally while we finish working the system out.
Anything here can be changed — see the last section — except the one thing
that says it cannot.*

## What this is

A place to chill and play games, 3a rawa2, without the headache of people
taking things way too seriously. No trolling, no toxicity, no insults, no
disrespect. We have a nice time.

It is not a community server, unless one day we decide we want it to be.

## What we are trying to do differently

The usual problem with servers is that everything gets read as levels and
power: owner, admin, mod, then everyone else, in a line. We are trying it
without the line.

Nothing here is a rank. The roles are just tasks people picked up depending on
their time and what they enjoy doing. We have the same goals, everyone's
opinion counts, and everything is up for discussion. The community comes first
and the people holding keys are here to keep it a safe place to hang out —
that is the entire job.

- **Rotating ownership.** There is no owner if we are all the owner. Discord
  forces one account to hold the server, so we rotate it between a few people
  who already know and trust each other. The owner's job is to *not* use what
  they hold.
- **Admins are technicians.** People who are good at the technical side and can
  handle permissions, settings and tooling so the server can have nice things.
  They do not outrank anyone on any decision. They take part like everybody
  else.
- **Moderators are the social ones.** People who read a room well and can tell
  what members are feeling or need in order to say what they want to say and
  feel safe doing it.
- **Members** do everything except server management and moderation. They vote
  in polls, speak at gatherings, and propose features, events and changes.

Together the first three are the **cooperative** — not "staff", because there
are no owners here. We are cooperating to have a nice time.

Being in the cooperative is picking up a chore, not getting promoted. You can
put it down whenever you like, no notice and no explanation.

## How we decide

Problems get raised, ideally with a suggested fix, and the fix is up for
scrutiny like everything else. Anyone in the cooperative can call for a
discussion about anything they think matters, at any time.

Then we vote. Small stuff passes easily; the bigger and more permanent
something is, the more of us have to agree. Most things should not need a vote
at all. Votes stay private — nobody ever finds out who voted which way — but
what was decided is always public.

The details live in the rules of procedure, which are ordinary rules and can be
rewritten whenever we want.

## The rules of the place

Members stay respectful of each other. Specifically:

1. No insults, and no racist, homophobic, or otherwise derogatory language.
2. No toxic behaviour. That includes "jokes" aimed at someone's looks,
   orientation, ethnicity, or body.
3. No memeing about other people, no gossiping, no being intentionally hurtful.

The server is **PG-13** for suggestive or mature content: don't say or do
anything you wouldn't want a kid to overhear.

And follow the Discord Terms of Service.

## Nothing to hide

Meetings happen and the notes get published — cooperative meetings where
everyone gets a turn and anyone can be criticised including whoever is running
it, and open server gatherings where members say what they actually think.

No topics are off the table. The point is to get better, not to blame people.

The idea is that the community ends up being the average of its members and
whoever is keeping it running. We provide the shape, everyone else fills it —
like a puddle is just water taking the shape of the hole.

## The server is not the community

The community is the people, and those friendships exist whether or not this
server does.

If the server is ever destroyed — a rogue owner, a stolen account, Discord
itself — we rebuild somewhere else, without whoever destroyed it. The
structure, the rules, the roster, the decisions and the records are all backed
up off the server by Eugene. The only thing genuinely at risk is message
history, and we accept that.

Because the worst case is survivable, trust here is cheap. The rules are
written on that basis.

## Everything can be questioned

All of it. The structure, the thresholds, the roles, the rules of procedure,
and this document. If the cooperative wants it changed, or if enough members
raise a real problem with how something is being handled, it gets discussed and
it can change.

**Except one thing.** The single immutable rule is the value underneath all of
it: *everyone is respected, anything can be discussed, and everyone respects
everyone else.* That cannot be amended, repealed or voted away.

How that value gets interpreted — where exactly the lines sit, how incidents
get handled — is an ordinary rule and changes like anything else. The value
stays. Its interpretation is ours to argue about.
